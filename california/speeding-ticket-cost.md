# How Much Is a Speeding Ticket in California?

If you just received a speeding ticket in California, the number on the citation is not the amount you will actually pay. California uses a "base fine" system where the posted fine is only a fraction of the total cost. After the state applies mandatory penalty assessments, surcharges, and court fees, the amount you owe is typically 4 to 5 times the base fine listed in the Vehicle Code. A ticket that shows a $35 base fine actually costs $238 out of pocket. And the fine is only the beginning -- when you factor in 3 years of insurance increases, a single speeding ticket in California can cost you well over $1,500.

This guide breaks down every component of California speeding ticket costs so you know exactly what to expect and what you can do to reduce the total damage.

---

## California Speeding Ticket Base Fine Schedule

California Vehicle Code sections 22349, 22350, and 22348 set the base fines for speeding violations. The base fine depends on how far over the posted speed limit you were driving.

| Speed Over Limit | Base Fine | Total with Assessments |
|---|---|---|
| 1-15 mph over | $35 | $238 |
| 16-25 mph over | $70 | $367 |
| 26+ mph over | $100 | $490+ |
| 100+ mph (VC 22348b) | $500 | $900+ |

These totals include all mandatory penalty assessments and surcharges applied by the state and county. The exact amount varies slightly by county because some counties impose additional local assessments, but the figures above represent the standard statewide totals for most California courts.

The 100+ mph violation under Vehicle Code 22348(b) is a separate offense that carries enhanced penalties beyond the fine, including a mandatory 30-day license suspension for a first offense and a 6-month suspension for a second offense within 3 years.

---

## Why the Total Is So Much Higher Than the Base Fine

California imposes over a dozen separate penalty assessments and surcharges on top of every traffic fine. These assessments are set by the state legislature and are mandatory -- judges cannot waive them.

Here is how a $35 base fine becomes $238:

| Assessment | Rate | Amount on $35 Fine |
|---|---|---|
| State penalty assessment (PC 1464) | $10 per $10 of base fine | $40 |
| County penalty assessment (GC 76000) | $7 per $10 of base fine | $28 |
| Court construction (GC 70372) | $5 per $10 of base fine | $20 |
| DNA identification fund (GC 76104.7) | $1 per $10 of base fine | $4 |
| State surcharge (PC 1465.7) | 20% of base fine | $7 |
| EMS fund (GC 76000.5) | $2 per $10 of base fine | $8 |
| State court construction (GC 70372a) | $5 per $10 of base fine | $20 |
| Conviction assessment (GC 70373) | Flat $35 per conviction | $35 |
| Court operations (PC 1465.8) | Flat $40 per conviction | $40 |
| Night court fee (if applicable) | $1 per $10 | $4 |
| **Total** | | **$238** |

The combined effect of these assessments is a multiplier of roughly 4.0x to 4.8x, depending on the base fine amount and county. Higher base fines result in proportionally higher assessment totals, which is why a $100 base fine (26+ mph over) reaches $490 or more after assessments.

This multiplier system means that California speeding tickets are among the most expensive in the nation, even though the base fines listed in the Vehicle Code appear relatively low.

## The Real Cost: Insurance Impact

The fine you pay to the court is only one part of the total cost of a California speeding ticket. For most drivers, the insurance increase is the largest financial consequence.

### How Much Does a Speeding Ticket Raise Insurance in California?

A single speeding ticket on your California driving record raises your auto insurance premium by an average of 20 to 30 percent. The increase takes effect at your next policy renewal after the violation appears on your DMV record, and it remains in effect for 3 years from the date of the conviction.

Here is what that looks like in dollars:

- **Average California annual auto insurance premium:** $2,200
- **Average increase after a speeding ticket:** 25% ($550 per year)
- **Duration of increase:** 3 years
- **Total insurance cost of one speeding ticket:** $1,650

For drivers with higher premiums, the cost is proportionally larger. If you pay $3,000 per year for insurance, a 25% increase adds $750 per year, or $2,250 over 3 years.

The insurance increase is based on the points added to your DMV record. A 1-point violation (1-25 mph over) triggers a standard rate increase. A 2-point violation (26+ mph over) triggers a larger increase, often 35 to 50 percent, because insurers view it as a more serious risk indicator.

### Why Traffic School Matters for Insurance

When you complete traffic school, the point is masked from your public DMV record. Insurance companies cannot see masked violations, so your rates do not increase. This is why traffic school is the single most cost-effective action you can take after receiving a speeding ticket in California. The $50 to $75 traffic school fee can save you $1,650 or more in avoided insurance increases.

## How to Avoid Paying the Full Cost

You have several options to reduce or eliminate the cost of a California speeding ticket. The right strategy depends on the severity of your violation, your eligibility, and how much effort you want to invest.

### Option 1: Attend Traffic School

Traffic school is the most common and reliable way to minimize the cost of a California speeding ticket. By completing a DMV-licensed traffic school course, you prevent the point from appearing on your public driving record, which keeps your insurance rates from increasing.

**Eligibility requirements:**

- Your violation is a 1-point infraction (speeding 1-25 mph over the limit)
- You hold a valid, non-commercial California driver license
- You have not attended traffic school for another ticket in the past 18 months
- The violation occurred in a non-commercial vehicle

**Cost:** You must pay the full fine plus an administrative fee of $50 to $75 (varies by county), plus the cost of the traffic school course itself ($19 to $49 for online courses).

**What you save:** The entire insurance increase, which averages $1,650 over 3 years.

For a complete guide to California traffic school, see our [California traffic ticket guide](/california/).

### Option 2: Trial by Written Declaration

California Vehicle Code section 40902 allows you to contest your speeding ticket by mail without appearing in court. You submit a written statement explaining your defense, and the citing officer submits a written response. A judge reviews both statements and issues a ruling by mail.

This option is worth considering because:

- Officers frequently fail to submit their written declaration, resulting in an automatic dismissal
- You can craft a detailed, carefully worded defense without the pressure of a courtroom
- If you lose, you can request a Trial de Novo (a new in-person trial) with no penalty

You must pay the full bail amount (the fine) upfront, but it is refunded if you win. If you lose the written declaration but win at Trial de Novo, you still receive a full refund.

### Option 3: Fight Your Ticket in Court

You have the constitutional right to contest any traffic citation in court. When you plead not guilty and request a trial, the citing officer must appear in court to testify. If the officer does not appear, the case is typically dismissed.

Even when the officer appears, you can present defenses such as:

- The speed measurement was inaccurate (radar calibration issues, incorrect visual estimate)
- The speed limit was not properly posted
- You were driving safely for conditions (VC 22350 defense for "basic speed law" violations)
- The officer misidentified your vehicle

Fighting a ticket in court requires more time and effort than traffic school, but a successful defense results in a complete dismissal with no fine, no points, and no insurance impact.

### Option 4: Request a Fine Reduction

If you cannot afford the full fine, California courts offer several forms of financial relief:

- **Payment plan:** Most courts allow you to pay in installments
- **Community service:** Some courts allow you to work off the fine through community service hours
- **Ability-to-pay reduction:** Under Penal Code 1385.1 and Vehicle Code 42003, you can request a fine reduction based on financial hardship. Courts may reduce fines by 50 to 80 percent for low-income drivers

## Speed Traps and Common Enforcement Locations

California has strict rules about speed traps under Vehicle Code sections 40801 through 40805. A speed trap is defined as a stretch of road where the speed limit is set lower than the prevailing speed of traffic without a valid engineering and traffic survey. If you can prove you were ticketed in a speed trap, the ticket may be dismissed.

### Where Speed Enforcement Is Heaviest

- **Construction zones:** Fines are doubled in active construction zones where workers are present. A $238 ticket becomes $476.
- **School zones:** Speed limits drop to 25 mph when children are present. The base fine for speeding in a school zone is the same as the standard fine schedule, but officers enforce these zones aggressively and judges are less sympathetic to defenses.
- **Highway patrol corridors:** The California Highway Patrol (CHP) focuses enforcement on major highways and freeways, particularly Interstate 5, Interstate 405, Highway 101, and Interstate 80. Speed enforcement is heaviest during commute hours and holiday weekends.
- **Downhill grades:** Several locations in California are known for speed enforcement on downhill stretches where vehicles naturally accelerate, including the Grapevine (I-5 through the Tehachapi Mountains), Highway 17 between San Jose and Santa Cruz, and the Altamont Pass on I-580.

### How to Check If a Speed Limit Is Valid

Under California law, speed limits on most roads must be based on an engineering and traffic survey conducted within the last 7 to 10 years. If the survey is outdated or was never conducted, the speed limit may not be enforceable through radar or lidar. You can request the traffic survey for the road where you were cited by filing a public records request with the local jurisdiction that controls the road.

## What Happens If You Do Not Pay Your California Speeding Ticket

Ignoring a California speeding ticket does not make it go away. The consequences escalate quickly:

### Within 20 Days of Your Court Date

If you do not respond to your ticket by the court date (known as the "courtesy notice" deadline), the court adds a **$300 civil assessment fee** to your existing fine under Penal Code 1214.1. Your $238 ticket just became $538.

### License Suspension

The court notifies the DMV of your failure to appear, and the DMV places a hold on your driving record. You will not be able to renew your license or vehicle registration. Driving on a suspended license is a separate misdemeanor offense under Vehicle Code 14601, which carries additional fines and potential jail time.

### Bench Warrant

The court may issue a bench warrant for failure to appear under Vehicle Code 40508. While officers rarely conduct active searches for traffic warrant holders, you can be arrested during any future traffic stop or contact with law enforcement. The warrant also appears on background checks.

### Collections

After a period of non-payment, the court may send your fine to a collections agency. This can damage your credit score and add collection fees to the total amount owed.

### How to Resolve an Overdue Ticket

If you have an overdue ticket, contact the court directly. Most California courts offer amnesty programs or payment plans for old tickets. You may be able to get the $300 civil assessment fee reduced or waived if you demonstrate financial hardship.

## Speeding Ticket Points and Your California Driving Record

Every speeding ticket conviction adds points to your California DMV driving record:

| Violation | Points | Duration on Record |
|---|---|---|
| Speeding 1-15 mph over | 1 point | 3 years |
| Speeding 16-25 mph over | 1 point | 3 years |
| Speeding 26+ mph over | 2 points | 3 years |
| Driving 100+ mph (VC 22348b) | 2 points | 7 years |

### Negligent Operator Threshold

California uses a point system to identify negligent operators. If you accumulate too many points, the DMV initiates a negligent operator hearing that can result in license suspension or revocation:

- **4 points in 12 months** triggers a hearing
- **6 points in 24 months** triggers a hearing
- **8 points in 36 months** triggers a hearing

This is another reason why attending traffic school matters. Masking even one point can keep you below the negligent operator threshold.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How much is a speeding ticket in California for going 10 mph over?

A speeding ticket for going 1 to 15 mph over the speed limit in California has a base fine of $35. After mandatory penalty assessments are added, the total you pay the court is approximately $238. If you do not attend traffic school, the true cost including 3 years of insurance increases averages $1,888.

### Why is my California speeding ticket so much more than the base fine?

California adds over a dozen mandatory penalty assessments and surcharges on top of every traffic fine. These include the state penalty assessment, county penalty assessment, court construction fee, DNA identification fund, emergency medical services fund, and several others. Combined, these multiply the base fine by approximately 4 to 5 times.

### How much does a speeding ticket raise insurance in California?

A single speeding ticket raises California insurance premiums by an average of 20 to 30 percent. With the average California annual premium around $2,200, a 25 percent increase adds approximately $550 per year. Since the increase lasts 3 years, the total insurance cost is approximately $1,650 on top of the fine.

### Can I go to traffic school for a speeding ticket in California?

Yes, if your ticket is for 1 to 25 mph over the limit (a 1-point violation). You must hold a valid non-commercial California license, the violation must be an infraction, and you cannot have attended traffic school in the past 18 months. Tickets for 26+ mph over are 2-point violations and are generally not eligible for traffic school.

### How much is a speeding ticket for going 100 mph in California?

Driving 100 mph or more is a separate offense under Vehicle Code 22348(b). The base fine is $500, and the total with penalty assessments is $900 or more. A first offense carries a mandatory 30-day license suspension. You cannot attend traffic school for this violation, and the insurance impact over 3 years can exceed $3,000.

### What happens if I do not pay my California speeding ticket?

The court adds a $300 civil assessment fee to your balance. Your license will be suspended, and the court may issue a bench warrant for failure to appear. The DMV will place a hold on your driving record, preventing you from renewing your license or registration until you resolve the matter.

### How many points does a speeding ticket add in California?

Speeding 1 to 25 mph over the limit adds 1 point to your DMV record. Speeding 26 or more mph over adds 2 points. Driving 100+ mph adds 2 points that remain on your record for 7 years instead of the standard 3 years.

### Is a speeding ticket a misdemeanor in California?

Most speeding tickets are infractions. However, certain speed-related offenses can be charged as misdemeanors, including reckless driving (VC 23103), speed contests or exhibitions of speed (VC 23109), and evading a peace officer at high speed (VC 2800.2). A misdemeanor carries potential jail time and a permanent criminal record.
