# How to Dismiss a Traffic Ticket in Virginia

Virginia is one of the toughest states in the country for traffic enforcement, and its laws work differently from almost every other state. If you received a traffic ticket in Virginia, you need to understand three facts before you do anything else:

1. **Reckless driving is a criminal misdemeanor** — not just a traffic ticket. Driving 20 mph or more over the speed limit, or exceeding 85 mph on any road, is a Class 1 misdemeanor carrying up to 12 months in jail.
2. **Virginia has no traffic school for ticket dismissal.** Unlike Florida, Texas, California, and most other states, you cannot take a course to make your ticket go away. Virginia's driver improvement clinic reduces points but does not dismiss tickets.
3. **Virginia uses a unique safe driving point system** that rewards clean driving and penalizes violations through demerit points tracked by the Virginia DMV.

These differences mean that strategies that work in other states, such as taking an online defensive driving course to dismiss a ticket, simply do not exist in Virginia. Your options are more limited, and the consequences of doing nothing are more severe.

This guide covers every option available to Virginia drivers: fighting your ticket in General District Court, negotiating with prosecutors, understanding the demerit point system, using the driver improvement clinic strategically, and knowing when you absolutely need a lawyer.

## Virginia's Reckless Driving Law: Why It Matters

Virginia's reckless driving statute is the single most important thing to understand about traffic law in the Commonwealth. What would be a simple speeding ticket in most states is a criminal charge in Virginia.

### What Qualifies as Reckless Driving

Under Virginia Code Section 46.2-862, reckless driving by speed is defined as:

- Driving **20 mph or more over the posted speed limit**, or
- Driving in excess of **85 mph** regardless of the posted speed limit

This means that on a Virginia interstate posted at 70 mph, driving 90 mph is reckless driving (20 over). But driving 86 mph on that same road is also reckless driving, even though you are only 16 mph over the posted limit, because you exceeded the 85 mph threshold.

Virginia Code Section 46.2-852 also defines reckless driving more broadly as driving "at a speed or in a manner so as to endanger the life, limb, or property of any person." Additional reckless driving offenses under Title 46.2 include passing on a curve, passing a stopped school bus, racing, driving with faulty brakes, and driving too fast for conditions.

### Penalties for Reckless Driving

Reckless driving is a **Class 1 misdemeanor** in Virginia, the same classification as DUI, shoplifting, and assault and battery. Penalties include:

| Penalty | Maximum |
|---------|---------|
| Jail time | Up to 12 months |
| Fine | Up to $2,500 |
| License suspension | Up to 6 months |
| DMV demerit points | 6 points (stays on record 11 years) |
| Criminal record | Permanent (unless expunged) |

Jail time is uncommon for simple speed-based reckless driving cases, but judges in some Virginia jurisdictions impose it for extreme speeds (typically 100 mph or higher, or 30+ mph over the limit). Even without jail time, a reckless driving conviction creates a permanent criminal record visible on background checks, which can affect employment, security clearances, and professional licensing.

### Why Reckless Driving Demands a Lawyer

Because reckless driving is a criminal charge, not a traffic infraction, you have far more at stake than a fine. A skilled Virginia traffic attorney can often negotiate a reckless driving charge down to:

- **Improper driving** (Virginia Code Section 46.2-869): A traffic infraction carrying a $500 maximum fine and 3 demerit points, with no criminal record. This is the best possible outcome short of dismissal.
- **Simple speeding**: A basic traffic infraction with no criminal implications.
- **Dismissal**: Rare, but possible if the officer does not appear, the speed measurement was flawed, or other procedural errors occurred.

Attorney fees for Virginia reckless driving cases typically range from $500 to $2,500, depending on the jurisdiction, the attorney's experience, and the complexity of the case. Given that a criminal conviction can cost tens of thousands of dollars in lost opportunities over a lifetime, hiring an attorney is almost always the financially sound decision.

## Prepayable vs. Non-Prepayable Offenses

Virginia divides traffic offenses into two categories that determine whether you must appear in court.

### Prepayable Offenses

Prepayable offenses are minor traffic infractions you can resolve by paying the fine and court costs before your court date. You do not need to appear in court. However, prepaying is legally the same as pleading guilty. The conviction goes on your DMV record, demerit points are assessed, and your insurance rates will likely increase.

Common prepayable offenses include:

- Speeding (under 20 mph over the limit and under 85 mph)
- Expired registration or inspection
- Running a stop sign or red light
- Failure to signal
- Following too closely
- Equipment violations (broken taillight, tinted windows)

### Non-Prepayable Offenses

Non-prepayable offenses require a mandatory court appearance. You cannot simply pay a fine to resolve them. These include:

- Reckless driving (any form)
- DUI / DWI
- Driving on a suspended or revoked license
- Eluding police
- Hit and run
- Any offense carrying potential jail time

If your ticket is marked "non-prepayable," you must appear in General District Court on your court date. Failure to appear results in a bench warrant for your arrest and an additional charge of failure to appear, which is itself a misdemeanor under Virginia Code Section 19.2-128.

### Should You Prepay a Traffic Ticket?

Just because a ticket is prepayable does not mean prepaying is the right choice. Prepaying means:

- Automatic guilty plea
- Demerit points on your record
- Likely insurance increase for 3 to 5 years
- Court costs of approximately $62 plus the fine

If the ticket carries 3 or more demerit points, the long-term insurance cost often exceeds $1,500. In many cases, appearing in court to contest the charge or negotiate a reduction saves money in the long run. ## Fighting Your Ticket in General District Court

All Virginia traffic cases are heard in **General District Court**, which is the trial court for traffic infractions and misdemeanors. Virginia does not have a separate traffic court. Every city and county has its own General District Court.

### The Court Process

1. **Arraignment**: For non-prepayable offenses, your first court date is an arraignment where you enter a plea. For prepayable offenses you chose to contest, your court date serves as both arraignment and trial.
2. **Trial**: If you plead not guilty, the judge schedules a trial (or conducts it the same day for simple cases). There are no jury trials in General District Court; the judge decides.
3. **Verdict**: The judge finds you guilty or not guilty, or may amend the charge to a lesser offense.
4. **Appeal**: If found guilty, you have 10 days to appeal to Circuit Court, where you receive a completely new trial (trial de novo), and you may request a jury.

### Plea Bargaining in Virginia

Virginia's approach to plea bargaining in traffic cases varies significantly by jurisdiction. Some key points:

- **Prosecutors are not present in every court.** In many Virginia General District Courts, there is no prosecutor for traffic cases. You deal directly with the judge. This means there is no one to negotiate a plea deal with before trial.
- **Some jurisdictions have traffic prosecutors.** Larger jurisdictions like Fairfax County, Virginia Beach, and Prince William County typically have prosecutors handling traffic dockets, which opens the door to pre-trial negotiations.
- **Judges can amend charges.** Even without a prosecutor, the judge may amend a reckless driving charge to improper driving or a basic speeding ticket after hearing the evidence. This is a common outcome, especially for drivers with clean records and speeds on the lower end of the reckless driving range.

### Common Defenses

- **Radar/lidar calibration**: Virginia law requires that speed measurement devices be calibrated within 6 months prior to the citation. The officer must present a valid calibration certificate in court. If the certificate is missing, expired, or improperly completed, the speed reading is inadmissible.
- **Officer certification**: The officer must be trained and certified to operate the specific speed measurement device used. Request proof of certification.
- **Speedometer calibration defense**: For reckless driving by speed, obtaining an independent speedometer calibration showing your speedometer reads lower than actual speed can persuade a judge to reduce the charge. This does not excuse the speed but demonstrates good faith and may show the actual speed was lower than alleged.
- **GPS evidence**: If you had a GPS device or navigation app running, the speed log may contradict the officer's reading.
- **Traffic flow defense**: You were keeping pace with the flow of traffic and changing speed would have created a hazard. This is not a legal defense per se, but judges sometimes consider it as a mitigating factor.
- **Emergency**: You were speeding due to a genuine emergency (medical crisis, imminent danger). This defense requires credible evidence.

### What to Bring to Court

- Your citation
- Speedometer calibration certificate (if obtained, typically $50 to $75)
- Any evidence supporting your defense (photos, GPS data, dashcam footage)
- Proof of completion of a driver improvement clinic (if completed before court, this can influence the judge's decision)
- Driving record printout showing clean history
- Proof of corrected equipment violations (if applicable)

## Virginia DMV Demerit Point System

Virginia's point system is managed by the DMV and works on a scale from +5 (safe driving points) to negative values (demerit points from violations). This system is distinct from other states because it rewards good driving with positive points rather than only penalizing bad driving.

### How Safe Driving Points Work

Virginia awards **1 safe driving point per calendar year** for each year you hold a valid Virginia driver's license without any violations or suspensions. The maximum balance of safe driving points is **+5**.

Safe driving points serve as a buffer against demerit points. If you have +5 safe driving points and receive a 3-point violation, your point balance drops to +2 rather than going directly negative. This means that a driver with a clean multi-year record can absorb a minor violation without reaching the danger zone for license suspension.

### Demerit Points by Violation

| Violation | Demerit Points | Duration on Record |
|-----------|---------------|-------------------|
| Speeding 1-9 mph over | 3 | 2 years |
| Speeding 10-19 mph over | 4 | 2 years |
| Speeding 20+ mph over (reckless driving) | 6 | 11 years |
| Reckless driving (any form) | 6 | 11 years |
| Running a red light | 4 | 2 years |
| Running a stop sign | 4 | 2 years |
| Following too closely | 4 | 2 years |
| Improper passing | 4 | 2 years |
| Failure to yield right of way | 4 | 2 years |
| Driving under the influence (DUI) | 6 | 11 years |
| Driving on suspended license | 6 | 11 years |
| Hit and run | 6 | 11 years |
| Improper driving | 3 | 2 years |

### Point Accumulation Consequences

| Point Balance | Consequence |
|--------------|-------------|
| -12 points | Mandatory attendance at a driver improvement clinic |
| -18 points | 90-day license suspension + driver improvement clinic |
| -24 points | 1-year license revocation |

### How Points Affect Insurance

Even a single traffic conviction in Virginia can raise your insurance premiums significantly. Virginia insurance companies review your DMV record at renewal and adjust rates based on violations. ## Virginia Driver Improvement Clinic

The Virginia driver improvement clinic is an **8-hour course** that adds **5 safe driving points** to your DMV record. This is the closest thing Virginia has to traffic school, but it works very differently from traffic school in other states.

### What the Clinic Does and Does Not Do

**The clinic does:**
- Add 5 safe driving points to your record
- Offset demerit points from violations
- Demonstrate good faith to a judge if completed before a court date
- Satisfy a court order if the judge mandates clinic attendance
- Help you avoid reaching the -12 point threshold for mandatory attendance

**The clinic does not:**
- Dismiss your traffic ticket
- Remove the conviction from your record
- Prevent insurance rate increases from the underlying conviction
- Erase demerit points (it adds positive points, which offset negatively)

This distinction is critical. In states like Florida or Texas, completing a [traffic school course](/traffic-school/) can dismiss the ticket entirely, removing it from your record as if it never happened. Virginia offers no such option. The conviction stays. The demerit points stay. The clinic simply adds positive points to your balance.

### How to Enroll

- You can take the clinic voluntarily (no court order needed) once every 24 months
- Virginia DMV maintains a list of approved clinic providers on its website
- Both classroom and online formats are available from DMV-approved providers
- The course takes 8 hours to complete
- Cost ranges from $40 to $100 depending on the provider and format

### Strategic Use of the Clinic

Even though the clinic does not dismiss tickets, it can be a valuable tool in specific situations:

1. **Before court**: Completing the clinic before your court date shows the judge you take the matter seriously. Judges in some jurisdictions will reduce charges or impose lighter sentences for defendants who have proactively completed the clinic.
2. **Point buffer**: If you have accumulated demerit points and are approaching the -12 threshold, the 5 safe driving points from the clinic can pull you back from the danger zone.
3. **After conviction**: If you plead guilty or are found guilty, the clinic points help offset the demerit points from the conviction, reducing the impact on your point balance.

## Virginia Speed Limits and Enforcement

Understanding Virginia's speed limits is essential because the margin between a simple speeding ticket and a criminal reckless driving charge is narrow.

### Posted Speed Limits

| Road Type | Speed Limit |
|-----------|-------------|
| Rural interstates | 70 mph (maximum allowed by law) |
| Urban interstates | 55-65 mph |
| Primary highways (non-interstate) | 55 mph (default) |
| Residential streets | 25 mph |
| Business districts | 25 mph |
| School zones | 25 mph (when flashing) |
| Unpaved roads | 35 mph |

### The Reckless Driving Speed Trap

The interplay between speed limits and the reckless driving threshold creates situations that catch drivers off guard:

- **70 mph interstate**: Driving 90 mph is reckless (20 over). Driving 86 mph is also reckless (over 85).
- **55 mph highway**: Driving 75 mph is reckless (20 over). But driving 76 mph is not reckless under the 85 mph threshold, only the 20-over rule applies here.
- **45 mph road**: Driving 65 mph is reckless (20 over).
- **25 mph residential**: Driving 45 mph is reckless (20 over).

Drivers from neighboring states accustomed to lighter speed enforcement are frequently surprised by Virginia's criminal reckless driving charges. On I-95 and I-81, out-of-state drivers make up a significant portion of reckless driving cases.

### Speed Enforcement Methods

Virginia State Police and local law enforcement use radar, lidar (laser), pacing, and aircraft speed enforcement. Virginia is one of only two states (along with Washington, D.C.) that bans the use of radar detectors. Under Virginia Code Section 46.2-1079, possessing a radar detector in your vehicle is illegal and carries a fine, and the device will be seized.

## Step-by-Step: What to Do After Receiving a Virginia Traffic Ticket

### For a Simple Speeding Ticket (Prepayable)

1. **Read your ticket carefully.** Note the court date, the charge, and whether it is marked prepayable.
2. **Decide whether to prepay or fight.** Use our [Decision Quiz](/tools/decision-quiz/) if you are unsure. Remember that prepaying equals a guilty plea.
3. **If fighting**: Appear in General District Court on your court date. Dress professionally. Arrive early. Plead not guilty and present your defense, or request a continuance to prepare.
4. **If prepaying**: Pay the fine and court costs before your court date online, by mail, or in person at the court clerk's office. Your guilty plea and demerit points are recorded immediately.
5. **Consider the driver improvement clinic.** Completing the 8-hour clinic adds 5 safe driving points to offset your demerit points, though it does not remove the conviction.

### For a Reckless Driving Charge (Non-Prepayable)

1. **Hire a traffic attorney immediately.** Reckless driving is a criminal charge. Do not represent yourself.
2. **Get your speedometer calibrated.** Take your vehicle to a certified mechanic for a speedometer calibration test. If your speedometer reads even 1-2 mph low, this evidence can help your attorney negotiate a reduction.
3. **Complete a driver improvement clinic before your court date.** This demonstrates good faith and responsibility to the judge.
4. **Gather evidence.** Dashcam footage, GPS logs, witness statements, and any documentation supporting your defense.
5. **Appear in court on your date.** You must appear personally for a non-prepayable offense. Your attorney can advise whether additional preparation is needed.
6. **Understand possible outcomes.** The best realistic outcome is reduction to improper driving (a traffic infraction, not a criminal offense). Dismissal is possible but uncommon. A guilty finding means a Class 1 misdemeanor criminal record.

## Important Deadlines

| Action | Deadline |
|--------|----------|
| Prepay a prepayable ticket | Before the court date on your citation |
| Appear in court (non-prepayable) | The court date on your citation |
| Appeal a General District Court verdict | 10 days after the court's decision |
| Complete court-ordered driver improvement clinic | Deadline set by the court |
| Pay fines after guilty finding | Typically due immediately or within 30 days |

### What Happens If You Miss Your Court Date

Missing your Virginia court date triggers serious consequences:

- The court enters a **guilty finding in your absence** for traffic infractions
- For criminal charges (reckless driving), the court issues a **capias** (bench warrant) for your arrest
- The DMV may **suspend your driving privileges**
- You may face an additional **failure to appear** charge under Virginia Code Section 19.2-128
- Additional fines and penalties are assessed

If you realize you will miss your court date, contact the court clerk before the date to request a continuance. Judges are far more willing to reschedule for someone who calls ahead than for someone who simply does not show up.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is reckless driving a criminal offense in Virginia?

Yes. Reckless driving in Virginia is a Class 1 misdemeanor under Virginia Code Section 46.2-862, the same criminal classification as DUI or assault and battery. A conviction carries up to 12 months in jail, a fine of up to $2,500, a license suspension of up to 6 months, and 6 DMV demerit points that remain on your record for 11 years. Driving 20 mph or more over the speed limit, or exceeding 85 mph regardless of the posted limit, qualifies as reckless driving.

### Can I take traffic school to dismiss a ticket in Virginia?

No. Virginia does not offer [traffic school](/traffic-school/) for ticket dismissal. Unlike states such as Florida, Texas, or California, completing a driver improvement clinic in Virginia does not dismiss your ticket or remove the conviction from your record. The 8-hour Virginia driver improvement clinic only adds 5 safe driving points to your DMV record, which can offset demerit points. The ticket and conviction remain.

### What is the Virginia safe driving point system?

Virginia uses a unique safe driving point system. You earn 1 safe driving point for each full calendar year you hold a Virginia license without any violations or suspensions, up to a maximum of +5 points. Completing a Virginia driver improvement clinic also adds 5 safe driving points (once every 24 months). Safe driving points offset demerit points. For example, if you have +5 safe driving points and receive a 3-point violation, your balance becomes +2.

### What speed triggers reckless driving in Virginia?

Under Virginia Code Section 46.2-862, you can be charged with reckless driving for exceeding the speed limit by 20 mph or more, or for driving faster than 85 mph regardless of the posted speed limit. For example, driving 76 mph in a 55 mph zone (21 over) or driving 86 mph on a highway posted at 70 mph both qualify as reckless driving.

### Do I need a lawyer for a Virginia reckless driving charge?

A lawyer is strongly recommended for any Virginia reckless driving charge. Because reckless driving is a Class 1 misdemeanor, a conviction creates a permanent criminal record that appears on background checks. An experienced traffic attorney may be able to negotiate a reduction to improper driving (Virginia Code Section 46.2-869), which is a traffic infraction rather than a criminal offense. Attorney fees for reckless driving cases in Virginia typically range from $500 to $2,500.

### How much are court costs for a Virginia traffic ticket?

If you are found guilty or prepay a traffic ticket in Virginia, court costs are approximately $62. This amount is set by statute and applies in addition to the fine for your specific violation. If you contest the ticket and are found not guilty, you do not pay court costs. Reckless driving cases may carry higher court costs because they are criminal misdemeanor proceedings.

### What is the difference between prepayable and non-prepayable offenses?

Prepayable offenses are minor traffic infractions you can resolve by paying the fine before your court date, which counts as a guilty plea. Most simple speeding tickets, expired registration, and equipment violations are prepayable. Non-prepayable offenses require a mandatory court appearance and include reckless driving, DUI, driving on a suspended license, and any charge carrying potential jail time.

### How long do demerit points stay on my Virginia driving record?

Demerit points remain on your Virginia driving record for 2 years from the offense date for 3-point and 4-point violations. For 6-point violations such as reckless driving and DUI, demerit points stay on your record for 11 years. The conviction itself may remain visible on your DMV record even after the demerit points expire.
