not a Texas quirk. Federal regulation 49 CFR 384.226 prohibits states from masking, deferring, or withholding a disqualification action for a serious traffic violation by a CDL driver, which is why the course route is unavailable to CDL holders. Read the rule at <a href="https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/49/384.226">49 CFR 384.226 (Cornell LII)</a>. Federal CDL disqualification thresholds for serious violations live at <a href="https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/49/383.51">49 CFR 383.51 (Cornell LII)</a>, and Texas DPS administers the state CDL program; see <a href="https://www.dps.texas.gov/section/driver-license">Texas DPS Driver License</a>.</p><h2>How the process works if you qualify</h2><p>If nothing blocks you, the sequence at a Texas justice court runs like this. You request the driving safety course from the court before your appearance date, enter a guilty or no contest plea, waive your right to a jury trial, show your valid Texas license and proof of insurance, and pay the court's course fee and administrative costs. The court grants or denies approval. After you complete a state-approved course you file the completion certificate with the court (<a href="https://www.jp.hctx.net/traffic/dsc.htm">Harris County JP courts, Driving Safety Course</a>).
