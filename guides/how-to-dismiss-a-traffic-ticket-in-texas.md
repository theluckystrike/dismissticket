# How to Dismiss a Traffic Ticket in Texas

**Texas issues millions of traffic citations each year.** The good news: Texas offers several straightforward paths to ticket dismissal, including defensive driving courses and deferred disposition. Here's everything you need to know.

---

## Option 1: Defensive Driving Course (Texas Education Agency Approved)

Texas allows eligible drivers to take a TEA-approved defensive driving course to dismiss a traffic ticket.

### Eligibility Requirements

- You hold a **valid Texas driver's license** (non-CDL for the violation vehicle)
- You have **not taken a defensive driving course** to dismiss a ticket in the past 12 months
- The violation is a **Class C misdemeanor** traffic offense (most standard tickets)
- You were **not in a crash that caused injury** at the time of the citation
- You were **not cited for speeding 25+ mph** over the limit
- You **do not hold a CDL** (unless driving a personal vehicle)

### How to Request It

1. **Contact the court** listed on your ticket before your appearance date
2. **Request permission** to take a defensive driving course (you can do this online for many Texas courts)
3. **Pay the court fee** — typically $100-$150 (covers court costs; the ticket fine is waived)
4. **Complete a TEA-approved course** — available online ($25-$50) or in-person
5. **Submit your completion certificate** and a certified copy of your driving record to the court within 90 days
6. **Case dismissed** — no conviction, no points, no insurance impact

### Cost

| Item | Amount |
|------|--------|
| Court administrative fee | $100-$150 |
| Online defensive driving course | $25-$50 |
| Certified driving record copy | $10 |
| **Total** | **$135-$210** |

Compare this to paying the fine ($150-$300+) plus the insurance increase ($1,200-$3,000 over 3 years).

## Option 2: Deferred Disposition (Probation)

Deferred disposition is like probation for traffic tickets. The judge delays your conviction for a set period (usually 90-180 days). If you stay violation-free during that period, the case is dismissed.

### How It Works

1. **Appear in court** or request deferred disposition online/by mail
2. **Judge sets conditions:** typically a fine (reduced), court costs, and a probation period
3. **Stay clean** — no additional traffic violations during probation
4. **Case dismissed** at the end of the probation period

### Advantages

- Works for violations where defensive driving is **not available** (e.g., second ticket within 12 months)
- No course required — just stay violation-free
- Shows as "dismissed" on your record

### Disadvantages

- You still pay a fine (usually the original amount or close to it)
- If you get another ticket during probation, **both tickets** are convicted
- Some insurance companies may still see the deferred disposition

## Option 3: Fight Your Ticket in Court

Every traffic citation in Texas can be contested with a not guilty plea.

### Process

1. **Enter a plea of not guilty** before your appearance date (in person, by mail, or online)
2. **Court schedules a trial** — bench trial (judge only) or jury trial (you choose)
3. **Officer must appear** to testify
4. **Present your defense** — you can bring evidence, photos, witnesses
5. **Judge or jury decides** — guilty or not guilty

### Common Defenses in Texas

- **Speed detection device not calibrated** — Texas requires radar/lidar to be calibrated and the officer to be certified
- **Signage issues** — speed limit sign not posted or obscured
- **Emergency necessity** — avoiding a collision or hazard
- **Mistaken identity** — officer pulled over the wrong vehicle
- **Due process violations** — officer didn't follow proper procedures

## Texas Traffic Violation Fines

| Violation | Fine Range |
|-----------|-----------|
| Speeding (1-10 mph over) | $150-$200 |
| Speeding (11-15 mph over) | $200-$250 |
| Speeding (16-20 mph over) | $250-$300 |
| Running a red light | $200-$275 |
| Running a stop sign | $200-$250 |
| No insurance | $260-$470 |
| Expired registration | $100-$200 |
| Cell phone in school zone | $200-$300 |

*Note: Texas does not use a point system. However, the Department of Public Safety tracks convictions, and accumulating violations can lead to surcharges and license suspension.*

## Important Deadlines

| Action | Deadline |
|--------|----------|
| Respond to ticket | **Before your appearance date** (printed on ticket) |
| Complete defensive driving | **90 days** from court approval |
| Submit driving record + certificate | **Before the 90-day deadline** |
| Deferred disposition probation | **90-180 days** (set by judge) |
